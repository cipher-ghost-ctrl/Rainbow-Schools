The client's foundational reference for every stage, session and storybook in the Scheme — transcribed here for the team to work from. Sits above any individual stage's working materials.

Vision

Why the Scheme exists.

Give every learner a fast, structured, genuinely joyful route into reading Punjabi — so that within 7 stages they can fluently read most Punjabi texts and begin to read Gurbani, without the year-long rote memorisation of the full 35-letter alphabet that discourages so many learners today.

Session Architecture

The 6-session template every stage follows.

Note: this differs from the 8-session structure seen in the original Stage 1 PDF guide — worth reconciling alongside the book-numbering conflict above.

SessionFocusCore activity
1Letter introduction through movementTrace large A5 cards; place around the room; run to the sound called out.
2Sound matching & memoryFace-up recall, then face-down "which one is...?" and a memory pairs game.
3Snap for reinforcementSmall cards played as Snap; the pile is only won by saying the correct sound.
4Add new lettersTwo remaining letters of the stage introduced and folded into the same games.
5Word-building funLetters physically joined into real and silly 2–3 letter words; discuss which are real.
6Reading words with SnapWord cards (not single letters) used; the learner reads each aloud to win it.
Storybook Architecture

The core production rule.

  • Controlled, decodable vocabulary — no word may appear in a storybook using a letter or matra the learner hasn't yet been taught, cumulatively, up to that point. The single most important production rule.
  • Fixed page pattern: cover → cast list → dialogue spreads with illustration notes → comprehension questions (4–5, always) → closing activities (re-read, Snap, build sentences, rebuild the book) → cut-out word/character cards at the back, duplicated in pairs.
  • Every real word checked against, and glossed exactly as given in, the Punjabi Dictionary published by Punjabi University, Patiala.
